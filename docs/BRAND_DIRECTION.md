# Brand direction

This is a practical voice and visual guide for the jev-classifier README. It is specific to this repository and does not adopt a reference project's brand.

## Audience feeling

A researcher should feel that the workflow is careful and inspectable. The copy should show where a claim came from and where the prototype stops. It should not promise that a model establishes scientific truth.

## Voice

- Use plain verbs: capture, classify, validate, record, render.
- Explain one technical term at the point it matters.
- Distinguish code that exists from planned evaluation.
- Keep citations near material claims.
- Use “signal” for a JEV bias answer and “evidence” for the cited source material.
- Avoid “autonomous researcher,” “verified truth,” “guaranteed,” and unqualified “bias detector.”

## Visual direction

Use an editorial research desk: warm paper, deep navy, muted blue, and restrained ochre connectors. Show one person reading real-looking paper sheets and one clear claim-to-record relationship. Keep typography sparse and visible. Images explain the source/evidence boundary; they do not illustrate model architecture or imply automatic integration.

The accepted images are assets/marketing/trace-each-claim.jpg and assets/marketing/keep-model-roles-clear.jpg. Their exact visible text, prompt intent, crop rules, review decisions, and hashes are in [IMAGE_GUIDE.md](IMAGE_GUIDE.md) and [IMAGE_NOTES.md](../assets/marketing/IMAGE_NOTES.md).

## Review limits

This is a declared design direction and editorial decision, not evidence that readers prefer these colors or illustrations. The [human review checklist](README_REVIEW.md) checks clarity and unsupported implications before delivery.
